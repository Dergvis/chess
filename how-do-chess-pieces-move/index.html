How Do Chess Pieces Move?

Rook: straight. Bishop: diagonally. Queen: straight or diagonally. King: one square. Knight: an L jump. Pawn: forward, captures diagonally. Practise free in CHEZZIES.

How does the pawn move?

The pawn walks forward and captures diagonally.

Pawns never move backwards. A pawn that is blocked by any piece directly in front of it cannot move forward, even if it could capture to the side. On the board, the dots show the two first-move squares and the rings show where the pawn could capture.

How does the knight move?

The knight jumps in an L and can hop over pieces.

The knight is the only piece that jumps over others. A handy check for kids: every knight move lands on a square of the other colour. From the middle of the board a knight has eight possible moves; from a corner, only two. That is why knights are stronger in the centre.

How does the bishop move?

The bishop slides along diagonals and stays on one colour.

A bishop that starts on a light square stays on light squares for the whole game. Each player has one light-square and one dark-square bishop, so together they can reach the whole board. A bishop cannot jump: any piece in the way stops it.

How does the rook move?

The rook slides in straight lines, forwards, backwards and sideways.

Rooks start in the corners and become strong once the board opens up. Two rooks working on the same row or column are a powerful team. The rook also takes part in castling, a special move with the king.

How does the queen move?

The queen moves like a rook and a bishop together.

The queen is the strongest piece, so beginners often bring her out too early, where she gets chased around. A good habit: before moving the queen, check whether an enemy piece could attack her on the new square.

How does the king move?

The king steps one square at a time and must never stay in check.

The king is not given points because the game ends when he is trapped. A king can never move into check, and two kings can never stand next to each other. Near the end of the game, when fewer pieces are left, the king becomes an active fighter.

What are the special moves?

There are three. Castling: the king moves two squares towards a rook, and that rook jumps to the other side of the king. Neither piece may have moved before, the squares between them must be empty, and the king may not castle out of, through or into check. En passant: if a pawn moves two squares and lands right beside an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square, but only on the very next move. Promotion: a pawn that reaches the far row becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight; most players choose a queen.

Which piece is the hardest to learn?

For most kids it is the knight, because it is the only piece that does not move in a straight line. Count it out loud: “one, two, turn, one”. A second trick: the knight always lands on a square of the other colour. Practise by placing a knight in the middle of an empty board and finding all eight squares it can reach.

What mistakes do beginners make?

Moving a pawn backwards or sideways. Capturing straight ahead with a pawn. Letting a bishop or rook jump over a piece; only the knight jumps. Moving the king into check, which is not allowed. Forgetting that a pawn on the last row must be promoted. In CHEZZIES the board checks every move, so an illegal move simply does not happen and your child can try again.

What are the point values of chess pieces?

A common guide is pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5 and queen 9. The king has no value because losing him ends the game. Points help with one simple question: is this trade good for me? Giving a knight (3) for a rook (5) is usually a good deal. They are a rule of thumb, not a law; a well-placed piece can be worth more.

Who moves first in chess?

White always moves first. Then the players take turns, one move each. You must move on your turn; you cannot pass.

Can a piece move through other pieces?

No, except the knight. Rooks, bishops and queens stop when a piece is in the way. They can capture an enemy piece that blocks them, but never their own.

Can the king capture?

Yes. The king captures like he moves: one square in any direction. He may not capture a piece that is protected, because that would put him in check.
